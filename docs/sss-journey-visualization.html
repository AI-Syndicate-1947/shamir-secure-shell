Demystifying Shamir’s Secret Sharing on your private key

This page walks through the encrypt-then-split design used in this repository: your key file is sealed with AES-256-GCM; only the 32-byte AES key is turned into Shamir shares over a prime field. The animation uses arithmetic modulo 17 so every step matches pencil-and-paper math (SHAMIR_SECRET_SHARING_MATH.md). The real tool uses the Mersenne prime 2521 − 1 instead — same ideas, bigger numbers.

Why a Galois field (finite field)?

Shamir’s construction needs a place where polynomials behave predictably: you want a unique interpolating polynomial of degree ≤ k−1 through any k distinct points. Over the integers ℤ, “division” is messy (no modular wrap). Over a field 𝔽p with p prime, addition, multiplication, and division by nonzero are all well-defined modulo p. That is exactly 𝔽p — the Galois field of order p. Nonzero elements have inverses, so Lagrange denominators like (xi − xj) never collapse to something non-invertible as long as x-coordinates are distinct. The secret s and every coefficient live in the same finite set, so “random polynomial” and “uniform uncertainty with fewer than k shares” are the standard SSS guarantees.

Changing k, n, or p regenerates coefficients. The story text always describes the general case.
